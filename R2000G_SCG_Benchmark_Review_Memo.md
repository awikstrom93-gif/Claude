# US Small Cap Growth Benchmark Review
### Why active managers underperformed the Russell 2000 Growth

**Prepared for:** Investment Committee  
**Scope:** Russell 2000 Growth (R2000G) vs. S&P SmallCap 600 Growth (S&P600G), 2011–2026

## Bottom line

Active US Small Cap Growth managers, benchmarked to the Russell 2000 Growth, lagged the index over the trailing 3 years (ending 2026-06). **This was structural, not a loss of manager skill.** The Russell 2000 Growth carries a large tail of unprofitable, often pre-earnings companies that a quality- or earnings-disciplined manager (whose portfolio resembles the S&P SmallCap 600 Growth) systematically avoids. That tail led the benchmark over the window managers were measured on. The same discipline *added* value over the full cycle (+45.2 pts) and carried lower volatility and drawdown.

---

## 1. The structural difference: R2000G carries a much larger low-quality tail

The S&P SmallCap 600 requires **positive trailing GAAP earnings** to enter the index; the Russell 2000 Growth has **no profitability screen**. That single rule produces a persistent quality gap:

| Measure (by index weight) | R2000G | S&P600G | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unprofitable (net income < 0), 2012 | 17.0% | 2.8% | **+14.2 pts** |
| Unprofitable (net income < 0), 2019 | 29.2% | 10.2% | **+19.0 pts** |
| Unprofitable (net income < 0), 2021 (peak) | 51.5% | 21.2% | **+30.3 pts** |
| Unprofitable (net income < 0), 2026 (latest) | 42.1% | 13.5% | **+28.6 pts** |
| **Never-profitable** weight, 2026 | **26.6%** | **1.8%** | **+24.8 pts** |

R2000G ran **10–32 points more unprofitable weight than S&P600G in every single year**. The sharpest cut is the *never-profitable* cohort (no profitable year on record): **26.6%** of R2000G by weight in 2026, versus **1.8%** in the earnings-screened S&P600G. R2000G's unprofitable weight peaked at **51.5% in 2021**, the height of the profitless-growth rally.

**Biotech is the embodiment of the gap.** Clinical-stage biotech is **57–92% unprofitable** and carries **up to 41% with no revenue at all**, so it is largely uninvestable for the earnings-gated S&P 600. It has been **7–17% of R2000G** (peaking at 17.4% in 2020) versus **2–7% of S&P600G** — a weight gap that widened to **~15 points** (2020). Over the 3-year window biotech contributed **+8.8 of the index's +66.2 points (~13% of the return) from ~12% of the weight**, and removing it from R2000G's own names cuts the window return to **59.6%** — roughly 7 points an earnings-disciplined manager would largely have missed. See the *Bio* tabs.

## 2. The realized cost: the unprofitable tail led the benchmark in the window

Decomposing R2000G's realized return into quality cohorts (point-in-time, Carino-linked so contributions sum to the index return):

- Over the **trailing 3-year window (ending 2026-06)**, the **never-profitable cohort was ~22% of R2000G by weight but delivered ~25% of the index's return** (+16.7 pts of the +66.2% total) — roughly **1.2× its weight**. The full unprofitable tail (fallen + never-profitable) drove ~36% of the window's return from ~33% of weight.
- A manager applying an earnings discipline would not have held those names — and would therefore mechanically lag the benchmark.

**The cleanest evidence is the counterfactual.** We rebuilt R2000G's *own* constituents as a "profitable-only" portfolio (the S&P 600-style screen, reweighted monthly):

| | Full period | Manager window (3 yr) |
|---|---|---|
| R2000G index (reconstruction) | 421.3% | 66.1% |
| Profitable-only (R2000G's own names) | **466.5%** | **57.5%** |
| Effect of screening for earnings | **+45.2 pts (helped)** | **-8.6 pts (hurt)** |

The sign flips. **Over the full cycle, screening for earnings *helped* by ~45 points; over the trailing 3-year window it *cost* ~9 points.** That window is precisely when active managers were judged.

A corroborating cross-check: the profitable-only rebuild's full-period return (**466.5%**) lands close to the *actual* S&P600G index return over the cycle (**486.2%**) — two independent constructions (an earnings screen on R2000G's own names vs. the separately-built S&P600G) landing in the same place, evidence the earnings screen is the mechanism rather than an artifact.

## 3. The long-run context: the discipline wins the cycle

Over the full 2011–2026 window the quality-screened index was the better asset:

| | R2000G | S&P600G |
|---|---|---|
| Cumulative total return | 406.1% | **486.2%** |
| Annualized return | 11.0% | **12.1%** |
| Annualized volatility | 19.9% | **18.4%** |
| Max drawdown | -33.4% | **-32.3%** |

S&P600G delivered more return with **less** risk across the cycle. Over the trailing 3-year window R2000G outran S&P600G by **+5.6 points (66.2% vs 60.6%)** on the back of its lower-quality tail — a reversal, not a durable regime change.
 Calendar years make the pattern concrete: R2000G led hardest in 2020 (34.6% vs 19.6%) and 2025 (13.0% vs 5.4%), but gave it back in 2021 (2.8% vs 22.6%) and 2016 (11.3% vs 22.2%).

**Why the window starts where it does — and a transparency note.** R2000G's cumulative excess over S&P600G bottomed in **2022-05** (its relative low); it has led the quality index since, and the trailing-3-year window sits within that recovery. The dates are not cherry-picked: the *Perf Window Proof* tab reports the gap across every candidate window, all ending 2026-06 — the benchmark’s lead is positive in each, so the conclusion does not depend on the exact start month.

## Implications for the Committee

1. **Reframe the underperformance.** Judged against R2000G, an earnings-disciplined SCG manager will lag whenever the benchmark's unprofitable tail leads. That is a benchmark-construction effect, not evidence of lost skill.
2. **Benchmark fit.** Where mandates are quality/earnings-disciplined, the S&P SmallCap 600 Growth is the more representative yardstick; consider it as a primary or secondary benchmark, or as context alongside R2000G.
3. **Forward view.** The full-cycle and risk numbers favor the disciplined approach. The recent window is the cost of that discipline during a low-quality rally, not a reason to abandon it.
